# Minnesota DNR recreation-trail source verification

Verified against official Minnesota DNR web pages, DNR metadata records, and DNR datasets published through the Minnesota Geospatial Commons and served by MnGeo's ArcGIS REST host. Live queries ran from 23:59 US Central on 2026-09-23 to about 00:20 US Central on 2026-09-24 (the session crossed midnight). Counts below are dated snapshots, not contractual totals. This note answers GitHub issue #15 and follows the same conventions as the earlier DNR service inventory (`dnr-recreation-service-inventory.md` on branch `research/dnr-services`).

Independently re-verified at about 18:10 US Central on 2026-09-24: the four layer schemas, anonymous queries, CORS, attribute distributions, Commons item metadata, metadata PDFs, and the DNR pages cited below all matched, with two exceptions. DNR updated the OHV and snowmobile datasets in between, so the OHV layer now has 8,044 features (8,033 hours earlier; its Commons item was modified 2026-09-24 20:06 UTC) and the metadata PDFs now show a "Time Period of Content" of 2026-09-23 for both OHV (2026-09-09 before) and snowmobile (2026-09-22 before). Counts below that are not marked otherwise are the earlier snapshot.

## Decision

All four resources have one authoritative, anonymous, queryable official FeatureServer: State Trails, snowmobile trails, off-highway vehicle (OHV) trails, and State Water Trails. All four are published by DNR Parks and Trails and hosted at `enterprise.gisdata.mn.gov`. None of them carries an open/closed, closure, grooming, or condition field, and none exposes a per-feature edit date. Closures and conditions exist only as human-facing DNR web pages, so they are link-only. The layers say where DNR records a trail and, with caveats, which uses it records; they must not be presented as a statement of current access or of what is legally permitted.

- **State Water Trails: integrate directly** under Fishing & water access. It is the cleanest fit: all 35 designated trails are present, geometry is static, and the metadata reads "Free for public use". It shows only a line, a name, and a program code, so hazards, campsites, and levels stay as DNR links.
- **State Trails: integrate directly** under Recreation trails, using only the fields the live service serves. The layer covers constructed trails only. It has no owner, status, or season field, and its use flags disagree with DNR's own trail list often enough that they need "recorded by DNR" wording. Trail alerts stay on the per-trail DNR pages.
- **OHV trails: integrate directly, season-gated.** The dataset carries a DNR-published season in its Commons title (`Minnesota OHV Trails, 2026 season`) and dated content metadata, plus vehicle-class flags and a DNR page link on 99% of features. Status lives only on the DNR OHV closures page.
- **Snowmobile trails: integrate directly, season-gated, and the least certain of the four.** The service publishes no effective period; the only currency evidence is an annually maintained content date in the Commons metadata plus DNR page statements about the September 1 to May 15 update window. The plan needs a MnMapping-verified season label and a freshness check (whether a stale layer becomes unavailable or only warns is a policy call, see "Seasonal gating"). If the plan will not carry that per-season check, downgrade this one resource to an official external link (DNR interactive map and the snow-depth/groomed-trail-conditions page).

Default all four off. Every popup and legend carries the reference-only wording below and a link to the matching DNR page for current conditions.

## Anonymous access tests

All calls were made without a token on 2026-09-23 23:59 CDT to 2026-09-24 00:16 CDT.

| State Trails | 200 | 978 | Twin Cities bbox `-93.6,44.7,-92.9,45.2`: 21 LineString features, about 223 KB |

| OHV | 200 | 8,033 | Twin Cities bbox: 0 features. Iron Range bbox `-93.0,47.3,-92.4,47.7`: 755 features, about 1.44 MB with 7 fields, 0.36 s |

Common findings for all four layers:

- `capabilities` is `Query,Extract`; `maxRecordCount` is 2000; `advancedQueryCapabilities.supportsPagination` is true (the OHV layer paged 8,033 rows in five `resultOffset` requests). The service root lists only `JSON`, but the layer lists `JSON, geoJSON, PBF` and both `f=geojson` and `f=pbf` returned 200.
- Native spatial reference is NAD83 UTM 15N (EPSG:26915); requests need `inSR=4326` and `outSR=4326`. Geometry is heavy (a 10-feature snowmobile response is over 1 MB), so viewport queries need `maxAllowableOffset` or quantization and pagination.
- Statewide `f=geojson` payloads measured 2026-09-24 (all attributes, `outSR=4326`): State Trails 3.9 MB (978 features, under the 2,000 cap), Water Trails 6.9 MB, snowmobile 35.9 MB (275 features). Snowmobile drops to 18.3 MB with three attributes and `geometryPrecision=5`, to 1.8 MB with `maxAllowableOffset=0.0005` (about 50 m), and to 1.2 MB at `0.002`; `f=pbf` with all attributes and no generalization is 5.3 MB. OHV exceeds the 2,000-record cap: one 2,000-feature page is 2.6 MB ungeneralized and 0.3 MB with `maxAllowableOffset=0.0005` and one attribute, and a northeast-Minnesota bbox (`-93,46.8,-91,48.2`) returned 1,422 features and 3.8 MB. Plan on generalized viewport queries and the "zoom in to load" hint for OHV.
- The response echoed the requesting `Origin` in `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` (tested with a placeholder origin), so a browser can query directly.
- `editingInfo` is absent and no layer has a date field, so there is no `lastEditDate` and no per-feature effective date.
- Each service root exposes its Commons item as `serviceItemId`, which the freshness check below can use.

The Commons item `modified` time is the ETL sync time, not a data edit date (the State Trails and Water Trails items moved from 2026-05-13 to 2026-09-23 with no content-stamp change). The dated evidence for currency is the metadata "Time Period of Content", read from the item metadata XML (`https://www.arcgis.com/sharing/rest/content/items/<itemId>/info/metadata/metadata.xml`) or the metadata PDF, not from the Commons search cache, which lagged for snowmobile and OHV. These platform endpoints are not documented Commons contracts, so use them in a release verification job, not at runtime.

Seasonal gating for the plan:

- OHV: accept only while the item title's year is the current calendar year or the next; otherwise the layer is unavailable. DNR published the 2026 dataset on 2025-12-04, so if it repeats that timing the next rollover is around December 2026.
- Snowmobile: take the season label from configuration (the season starting in the year of the most recent September 1), and treat the layer as unavailable if the metadata content stamp is missing or earlier than that September 1. Between May 15 and September 1 DNR says the data reflect the previous season, which this rule also labels as the previous season.
- State Trails and Water Trails do not need a season label.
- This gate extends the fail-closed rule beyond the four season-specific hunting layers, which [the DNR Recreation information architecture](https://github.com/eisch067/MnMapping/issues/10) limited to deer, CWD, bear, and turkey. The evidence supports a freshness label on OHV and snowmobile (DNR titles the OHV dataset by season and says the snowmobile data may be stale outside September 1 to May 15), but neither layer's geometry carries an effective period the way a hunting zone does. Whether a stale OHV or snowmobile layer should become unavailable or only show its content date with a warning is an owner policy call for delivery planning, not a source limitation.

## Coverage and other candidates

- **State Trails.** Constructed trails only; the metadata says "Proposed alignments and trail segments not yet built are not included." Minn. Stat. 85.015 authorizes more trails than the layer contains (for example Stagecoach, Prairie Wildflower, Superior Vista, and Des Moines River Valley do not appear by name), and no official planned-state-trail dataset exists in the DNR service directory or the Commons search. Seven of the 38 names (about 185 of 1,479 layer miles) are not on DNR's A-Z list (Alborn-Pengilly, Blue Ox, Blufflands Preston to Forestville, Cloquet-Saginaw, Dakota Rail, Gandy Dancer, and an "OHV access to Taconite" connector); four (Alborn-Pengilly, Blue Ox, Cloquet-Saginaw, Gandy Dancer) carry OHV program codes in `alt_name`, and Dakota Rail has no use flag set. The layer total (1,479 mi) is close to but not identical with DNR's "over 1,500 miles" headline and the A-Z table (about 1,418 mi). The broader [Minnesota Trails: Division of Parks and Trails](https://enterprise.gisdata.mn.gov/aghost/rest/services/us_mn_state_dnr/trans_state_park_trails_roads/FeatureServer/0) layer (26,963 features) contains 979 rows coded `TRA` (the state trails) plus state park roads, grant-in-aid club trails, and 6,526 rows coded `OHV`; its own record says it "is known to contain some geometry and attribute errors and is not guaranteed to be accurate" and to use it "at a scale of 1:24,000 or smaller". Do not use it as the state-trail source.
- **Snowmobile.** One statewide dataset covers all current trails regardless of funding. DNR states "over 22,000 miles of groomed snowmobile trails", with the GIA system "over 21,000 miles"; the layer sums to 23,102 and 21,455 (GIA). The state-park trails layer has no snowmobile flag in its live schema. County services on the Commons (for example Aitkin County grant-in-aid) are local and not authoritative statewide.
- **OHV.** The 106 trail names match DNR's description of "over a hundred state and grant-in-aid trails". The park-trails layer also carries 6,526 rows coded `OHV` in `prgm_prj`; use `trans_ohv_trails_mn`. DNR state forest roads and federal routes are separate and not part of this layer.
- **Water Trails.** All 35 statutory water trails are present; the layer's 4,508 miles agrees with DNR's "over 4,500 miles" (its recommended-paddling page says 4,375 designated river miles, so exact mileage is not reconciled). Some segments are slivers (a Minnesota River segment returned by the Twin Cities bbox test is about 1.4 m long).

## Licensing and attribution

The exact text on each Commons item and metadata record:

- State Trails: "Use of the data are constrained only by the DNR GIS Data License Agreement. See the Distribution Liability section below for a link."
- Snowmobile and OHV: "Specific Constraint - This dataset is not to be used for navigation and is a reference layer only."
- Water Trails: "Free for public use, cannot be sold or used as legal information and should be understood as an approximation of the water trail location."
- Access constraint is "None" for State Trails and Water Trails and "Open to public use, no restrictions" for snowmobile and OHV. All four metadata records point to the DNR General Geographic Data License Agreement in their Distribution Liability field. The service `copyrightText` fields are empty and the Commons license type is `custom`.

The [DNR General Data and Software License Agreement](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/sitetools/data_software_license_plain.html) grants "a personal, non-exclusive, non-transferrable, limited license to extract information from, use, reproduce, distribute, and modify data", allows derivative works, but says data "in its entirety may not be resold, distributed or displayed for commercial purposes or otherwise". It requires that "an MNDNR copyright notice will be included, as appropriate; or MNDNR must be acknowledged as having contributed to any derivative work", gives no right to DNR names, marks, or logos, and says map data "should not be interpreted as establishing or describing legal boundaries or legal access and should not be used for navigational purposes."

Attribute as `Minnesota Department of Natural Resources` (the Commons `source` on these items is DNR Parks and Trails), without DNR logos. For the current private personal program, live queries with attribution and the reference-only disclaimer fit the license. The phrases "Free for public use" and "Open to public use, no restrictions" lean permissive but sit alongside the DNR license, so they should not be read as a grant to display or redistribute an entire layer in a public, paid, or offline product. A later public release needs DNR confirmation, as the earlier DNR inventory already concluded.

## Carry into the plan

- Code to the live schema. The metadata lists fields the service does not serve (`admin`, `maintained`, `unit_name`, `use_snowmo`, `use_xski`, `use_ada`, `CLUB_NAME`) and omits ones it does (`owner_type`, `maint_by`, `stud_restr`). Treat the live field list as the contract and re-check it in the release verification job.
- Add a static, MnMapping-maintained table linking each State Trail name (30 on the DNR A-Z page) and each water trail name (35; Lake Superior lives under `/kayaking/lswt/`) to its DNR page, because the layers hold no page URL. `prgm_prj` matches the DNR page photo tag (`tra00730`, `cbr00603`) for the two checked. OHV features carry `web_site`; snowmobile `trail_num` is the number used on the DNR trail-contacts page.
- Snowmobile contact fields name individual volunteers with phone numbers and email addresses. They are labelled "public" by DNR; prefer linking the DNR trail-contacts page over rendering personal contacts.
- Use a release job to enforce the season gates above from `serviceItemId` and the item metadata; record source access date and the metadata content date on each layer definition, as for the hunting layers.
- Keep closures, grooming, levels, and alerts as links to the DNR pages named above. Do not scrape them or the DNR interactive maps, and do not derive open/closed from the data.
- Join water trails to the existing water-access layer through `state_water_trail_id` to show access points along each trail.

## Not verified

- No documented public data service exists for OHV closures, snowmobile grooming, state-trail alerts, or river levels for water trails; the pages were read only to characterize them, not harvested. Whether DNR publishes any such feed elsewhere was not established.
- The meaning of the Commons "Time Period of Content" stamps rests on DNR's metadata wording ("the date which the user can be confident of accuracy and completeness") and the observation that DNR, not the ETL, changes it for snowmobile and OHV. DNR does not say the stamp marks a season rollover.
- Hazards, rapids, and watercraft campsites appear on DNR's interactive water-trail map; whether they exist as an anonymous DNR service was not verified.
- The state-trail use-flag spot check used approximate name matching and DNR's icon list, so its 19 of 180 figure is indicative.
